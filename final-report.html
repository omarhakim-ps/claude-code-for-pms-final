Rook Dispatch Release 4.2 · Final report

Four responders went quiet.
Here's how we bring them back.

Release 4.2 cut the ping wait from 90 to 60 seconds. Misses jumped, and the system counts a miss like a refusal, with no way back. Four responders slid to the bottom of the ranking and their work went to people further away. Nobody was told. I'd keep the 60 seconds and fix everything around it, and I've checked that plan against all 16 responders, not just those four.

What I'd do about it

Keep the 60 seconds. Fix what happens inside and after them.

Seven changes in two releases, each shipped separately so we can measure what each one does. Routing settings ship globally, so we can't pilot them by area.

Release A Make a quiet week visible and a ping easier to catch

1

Visibility. Live pings with Nudge, each responder's week at a glance, a page with ranking over time, alerts, an availability switch, and a handler phone app.

2

Harder to miss. A full-screen countdown that gets urgent in the last 20s, and one-tap Take from the lock screen.

7

Report a problem where it happened. One ticket per ping with its record attached, plus a general form.

Release B Stop one bad week from becoming permanent

3

Miss penalty cut from −0.12 to about −0.04. No penalty for turning down a ping outside your area.

4

Make the four whole. A one-time restore of scores to their 11 Aug values.

5

Slow recovery. Scores drift back to 0.5 over about 4 weeks while a responder is available.

6

One retry. A missed local ping comes back once if the next responder doesn't take it.

Projection = brief's central case (alerts win back three quarters of the 60s miss increase). Callouts filled and the quiet four's share recover in every scenario. Answered and taken depend on the alerts working, which is why Release A ships first.

Why it's on the right track

  • ✓
    Nobody ends up below their own normal. The six who gained lose 3–6 pings a week each, back to their usual week. The middle six move by under 3.
  • ✓
    The whole base does better. About 123 pings taken a week, up from 111, and answered goes from 84.9% to about 92%. Even the pessimistic case (66% taken, 87% answered) beats today.
  • ✓
    Gainers mostly give up other areas' work. Their pings in their own area fell from about 80% to 50–60% after 4.2, so going back brings them closer to home.
  • !
    Misses went up for everyone, not just the four: gainers went from 0–4% missed to 10–17%. The alert in Release A is for the whole base.
  • !
    The quiet four stay the weakest, at 58–72% taken in the central case (69–82% before 4.2). Their numbers rest on 14–20 pings each.

If the plan doesn't work: what we'd watch and what we'd do

Contingency 1 · Release A

Release A makes no difference to misses

Signal: 3 full weeks after Release A, pings answered is still at or near today's 84.9%, so the weekly outcome review marks it Off track (below the brief's 85% floor; target 91.5%).

What it means: the problem is the 60-second window itself, not visibility. The brief's premise of keeping 60s needs revisiting.

What we'd do: test 90s on its own in a separate release, using the answer times we start logging now. That is a deliberate change to the ping wait, so we agree it first and update release-qa's agreed value; otherwise the skill blocks it. Release B still ships: it targets callouts filled and the quiet four's share, which don't depend on the alerts.

Contingency 2 · before Release B

The out-of-area jump turns out to be a separate bug

Signal: Wen finds that the jump in out-of-area first pings (20% → 51% on 12–14 Aug, before any scores fell) wasn't caused by the score or weighting changes.

What it means: fixing that bug may matter more than the score changes. Without it, the four get their volume back but the gainers keep being sent across areas.

What we'd do: rescope Release B around the bug fix first, then re-run these projections. Release A doesn't depend on it.

Recommendation Approve Release A now. Approve Release B once Wen has answered and the 11 Aug scores for the restore are confirmed.

What happened, plainly

A shorter wait, then a trap with no exit.

The pings moved first, the tickets followed a few days later. The thread said "since 18 Aug". The data says it started on release day.

60s wait→ Miss→ −0.12, same as saying no→ Ranked lower→ Fewer, further pings→ Only a taken ping lifts the score ↺ and there's no recovery over time. Farlight's score hit exactly 0 by 24 Aug.

Root cause (4.2)

The shorter ping wait. Strongest explanation, not proven: the ranking change shipped the same day and answer times aren't logged.

Already there before 4.2

A miss costs the same as a refusal. Scores never recover on their own. Out-of-area turn-downs cost points too.

Knock-on effects

Pings moved to six responders (41% → 61% of all pings). Unfilled callouts doubled (5.1% → 10.8%), all on a miss.

My prompt Module 4 · #19
Find me the part of this code that takes points off somebody when they miss a ping or turn one down. Show it to me and explain it in plain English. Then find me every single thing in this code that puts points back on.

The answer: one thing puts points back, and it's taking a ping. A responder at 0 needs seven taken pings to get back to 0.5, but at 0 they barely get pinged. At the 4.2 weights, a score of 0 vs 1 is worth about 19 minutes of travel time.

06 · Sidekicks Keeping it from happening again

4.2 shipped clean. Nothing was checking the outcomes.

I built two skills that run every week, one before each release and one after, so the next routing change gets checked against the four outcomes before it ships and measured after.

release-qa

Diffs the code against the last shipped release. Flags each lever that moves the four outcomes: Block, Warn, Pass, Not in code yet.

"Mark 4.3 as shipped"

Moves the baseline, so next week compares against what's really live.

Weekly, after

weekly-outcome-review

One query and a dated report: On track, Watch or Off track against the brief's projections, pooled over 3 full weeks.

If it drifts

Act early
